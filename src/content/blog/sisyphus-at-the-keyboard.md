he universe is fundamentally chaotic and indifferent, yet we spend our lives writing code that demands absolute order. Engineering is an inherently optimistic pursuit. It operates on the assumption that problems have solutions, that logic dictates outcomes, and that systems can be optimized. But what happens when the engineer steps away from the terminal and looks at the world? The rigid determinism of a script sharply contrasts with the unpredictable absurdity of human existence. Perhaps the programmer debugging the same elusive error for the hundredth time is the modern equivalent of Albert Camus’s Sisyphus, pushing his boulder up the mountain only to watch it roll back down. We build structures, squash bugs, and refactor code, imposing temporary order on a universe that tends toward entropy. We must imagine the engineer happy.
